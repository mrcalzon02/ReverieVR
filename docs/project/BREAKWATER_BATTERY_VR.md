# Breakwater Battery VR — canonical native game specification

**Status:** Development-only VR prototype, physical Galaxy S9 acceptance pending. **Backlog:** RV-0640. The authoritative implementation is in app/src/main/jni/native/breakwater_campaign.{h,cpp} and breakwater_module.cpp. This game uses original assets/code and does not import Beach Head content.

## Pitch and campaign structure

A stationary coastal-defense gun battery faces a gradually escalating amphibious invasion. The player is seated, scans the horizon by turning their head, and aims the gun using the shell-calibrated handheld pointer. No artificial walking or jolting camera recoil.

The campaign has three defended locations: **Shingle Bay (two days), Iron Quay (three days), and Blackcap Island (four days)**. Every day and night is a separate wave period with a shop after **every** wave. Initial Casual/Regular/Veteran/Siege wave schedules per day are 2/3/4/5 and per night 1/2/3/4, increasing on later maps and days with a ceiling of eight per period.

Briefing → inter-wave Shop → Combat → Shop → next wave → sunset/night → sunrise/day → next location → Victory. Integrity falling to zero means Defeat. Nothing automatically skips the shop.

The native input contract permits frame deltas up to 0.10 seconds. The campaign now advances that time in bounded 0.05-second substeps, rather than silently dropping half the elapsed time at 10 FPS. This preserves boat movement, spawn cadence, reloads and hostile attack timing on slow frames, while capping long pause/resume catch-up to 0.10 seconds. Deterministic native regressions compare one slow frame against two half-frames, including offshore gunboat salvos. Headset frame-pacing acceptance remains outstanding.

## Enemy transport rules

**The opening enemy is a rowboat, not a robot.** A rowboat carries exactly **one infantryman**. Stronger military landing craft appear later, each carrying **three to six infantry**. Ships have real position, speed and hull HP. **Sinking the craft before it beaches cancels every passenger**, while a surviving craft unloads its passengers as separate hostile shore infantry. Each passenger has an individual in-wave soldier slot and world position derived from its landing craft, advances from the surf toward the parapet, and can only be killed by an actual ray hit against that soldier. The old generic beach-region auto-kill is removed. Infantry only damage the battery after individually reaching the forward firing line at z = -1.34; while they are advancing across the beach, their attacks are disabled. The four-second salvo countdown begins when at least one living soldier reaches that line, and killing every ready shooter resets it. Each salvo's damage scales only with soldiers actually at the firing line, not all landed soldiers. Later progression introduces gunboats and overhead aircraft. Gunboats fire from offshore once within eight world units, dealing three integrity damage per salvo every 2.8 seconds. Surviving gunboats still cause eight integrity damage upon reaching the shore. Each simulated salvo has a short procedural muzzle flash and its own wave-status counter; destroying a gunboat before it reaches firing range prevents that bombardment. Aircraft now resolve as deterministic **strafers** or **bombers**: strafers pepper the battery repeatedly during one fast overhead pass, while bombers make a slower approach and deliver one heavier attack before departing. Destroying the aircraft before its attack window prevents that damage. Aircraft require the anti-air upgrade for gun damage or a purchased airstrike; firing the basic gun at one still consumes ammunition but cannot harm it. Enhanced AA tiers and more elaborate flight geometry remain planned.

The module generates simple cuboid rowboats, military boats and individually positioned shore infantry silhouettes (body and head), plus distinct generated bomber/strafer silhouettes. Rendered infantry positions and bounded combat-ray hitboxes share the campaign's authoritative soldier objects, and defeated soldiers are removed individually. Combat targeting now selects the nearest positive ray-surface intersection rather than hitbox-center depth for overlapping vessels, aircraft and infantry. The shared deterministic intersection helper rejects malformed rays; Galaxy S9 pointer alignment and headset hitbox proof remain outstanding.

## Campaign credits and purchases

Players earn credits for destroyed hulls, eliminated shore infantry and surviving a wave. Purchasing is allowed only between waves; upgrades have real effects in the simulation.

| Choice | Base cost | Existing behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| Fire rate | 180 | 35 ms less shot delay per tier; maximum 4 tiers |
| Damage | 200 | +10 impact damage per tier; maximum 4 tiers |
| Clip size | 160 | +4 rounds per tier; maximum 4 tiers |
| Anti-air | 750 | Unlocks ordinary gun engagement of aircraft |
| Artillery strike | 260 | One consumable bombardment of surface vessels |
| Airstrike | 420 | One consumable strike against surface or air |
| Support turret | 470 | Automatically damages nearest surface craft at a fixed cadence |
| Repair | 140 | Restores 25 battery integrity, capped at 100 |

Advanced weapon mounts, separate caliber variants and impact/splash effects remain later features; the current lightweight gun tracer is generated procedurally and purchases affect the actual C++ gameplay calculations, not merely their labels.

## First playable controls and 3DoF contract

The Development-only module is launched from Native Apps after setting launcher logging to Development. The shell retains Back, Home, Quick Menu and recenter control. The gun is aimed with the normalized native pointer direction.

In the shop, point at any of the eight labelled upgrade cards and press **Select** to buy it. Point at **START** and press Select to begin the next wave; an available secondary action can also begin it. In combat, point at a hull or an individual disembarked infantry silhouette and hold Select to engage; the nearest actual ray-intersected live target is selected, while aiming into empty sand spends a round as a miss. Fire cadence depends on purchased rate. Every valid in-traverse gun shot now consumes a round whether it hits or misses. A shot aimed at aircraft before anti-air is purchased still spends ammunition and produces feedback but cannot damage the aircraft. Point at **ARTY** or **AIR** and press Select to use a stockpiled strike; each combat HUD label displays its remaining charges. Artillery requires an active surface vessel and airstrikes require at least one active enemy (surface or aircraft). An empty-target call fails with the shell's rejection feedback without spending a charge, including when only infantry have already landed. The turret fires automatically after being purchased. The combat HUD also predicts the earliest real aircraft strike and gunboat salvo up to six seconds ahead, displaying separate AIR RAID and GUNBOAT countdowns. These warnings use live attack cooldowns, movement speeds, and actual firing windows; a destroyed attacker, exhausted bomber, or ship whose cooldown expires after it leaves the firing lane does not generate a false warning. Native regression assertions cover initial approach, post-attack and destroyed-target cases; headset legibility remains unverified. Each actual turret shot now records a deterministic simulation event with the target's pre-destruction coordinates and whether the hit was lethal. The native renderer consumes this event once per update, briefly flashes the turret muzzle and draws a metal impact or an expanding explosion for a destroyed vessel; idle turrets and unpurchased turrets do not fabricate hits. Regression coverage exercises a real first turret hit, a lethal follow-up shot, the original target position, and invalid effect coordinates. Headset appearance remains a device acceptance gate. Invalid actions produce the existing shell-owned failure cue. Victory/Defeat provides an implemented restart. Phone test must prove readable target silhouettes and reliable pointer hitboxes; this is not physical validation.

The gun carriage is world-anchored and the rendered barrel follows the handheld pointer only inside a finite traverse/elevation envelope; firing outside that mount arc is rejected. Successful gun shots create a short-lived generated tracer ribbon, including misses, while the 1.15-second automatic magazine reload makes clip-size upgrades meaningful. In combat, a secondary-button press now requests a tactical reload of a partially spent magazine using the same 1.15-second simulation timer. Full magazines, active reloads and non-combat phases reject the request without changing ammunition; shooting remains blocked until completion. The combat HUD shows SECONDARY RELOAD when a partial magazine is ready for manual reload, with the existing RELOADING progress and READY completion cues taking precedence. Native regression assertions cover the request lifecycle and rejection cases. During reload, the combat HUD displays a progress bar and RELOADING label, driven by the simulation timer and updated once before stereo rendering; firing remains blocked until the magazine refills. A fixed pool of eight procedural impact pulses produces sparks only for actual vessel/aircraft damage, infantry dust and sea splashes for downward misses. Lethal gun hits on hulls or aircraft now produce longer expanding smoke-and-fire explosions; rounds that cannot damage aircraft without the anti-air upgrade consume ammunition but no longer invent hit sparks. Impact positions are captured before lethal hits deactivate targets, and the pulses advance once per update rather than separately per eye. Horizontal and upward misses do not invent sea splashes. Native regressions cover damage-gated impact kinds, blast lifetime, pulse position, expiry, malformed input and capacity; headset visibility remains unverified. Artillery and airstrikes now emit separate, longer-lived procedural blast pulses for each actually damaged enemy (up to the fixed eight-pulse capacity), using pre-strike coordinates so sinking boats and destroyed aircraft still explode at the correct location. Artillery flashes amber at the waterline; airstrikes use a pale overhead flash for aircraft and surface targets. Rejected support calls and artillery-immune aircraft produce no false blasts. Native regression fixtures cover both support types, target locations, lifetime, and rejected calls; physical headset visibility is still unverified. The combat HUD now briefly shows READY for 0.55 seconds when the simulation confirms that the magazine has finished reloading; this cue does not trigger on ordinary idle frames or when combat has already ended. Audible weapon report/reload, enhanced AA tiers and more elaborate aircraft flight geometry remain explicit next slices. An 80-byte versioned shop-state save preserves completed waves and bought upgrades through the module-private host save service. Mid-wave exit resumes at the prior shop; S9 exit/re-entry and APK update durability remain device gates.

## Runtime art and visuals

Follow the tiny pre-PlayStation, early-PC 3D aesthetic: simple vertex-color meshes, procedurally striped sea, day/night palette, chunky silhouettes and a 3×5 generated pixel-card text renderer. Future recipe-driven additions: landing ramps, wakes, smoke/explosion cards, weather, sky/cloud noise, per-location terrain, procedural RGB565 material atlas, distant fog and sprite-based effects. No large texture packs, modern PBR, heavy shaders, imported licensed game materials or duplicated per-eye scene simulation.

## Original MIDI-era music for every native game

Soundtracks should evoke **early Sierra MIDI composition** through new melodies and lightweight note-event sequences, not copied tunes or proprietary instrument banks. Breakwater cues: harbor brass title, shop intermission, daytime battle, night assault, aircraft-warning sting, victory/defeat. Red Ledger: weary smoky noir. Lantern Desk: chamber-intelligence tension. Forward Detachment: measured snare and low winds.

**Actual native MIDI playback does not yet exist.** The current module has host-owned feedback cues only. The shared note-event sequencer, software synthesis and audio-focus service must be implemented and measured on the S9 before calling music functional. Reference: docs/native/MIDI_MUSIC_STANDARD.md.

## Acceptance gates

1. Native C++ tests for boat HP, 1-passenger rowboat, 3–6 military landing party, prevented landing, individual infantry allocation/advancement/ray-hit discrimination, infantry danger, upgrade behavior, turret and anti-air restrictions.
2. Android ARMv7 + ARM64 NDK build and signed installable phone-test APK, verified by remote read-back.
3. Independent Development-only launcher list and runtime gating.
4. Galaxy S9 + Daydream: stereo, seated comfort, shop Select, held fire, real boat sinking, shore-landings, day/night progression and emergency shell recovery.
5. Campaign save/update durability, actual projectile effects, audio/MIDI ownership, 15-minute thermal/frame pacing and battery test.

Do not promote the prototype to the normal launcher until the hardware gates have been met.

### Impact pool saturation behavior

The eight-slot impact pool prefers expired entries, then active minor effects. Minor sparks, dust, and splashes cannot evict live explosions when the pool is full; fresh explosion and support-blast effects may replace existing blasts when necessary. This is deterministic and allocation-free. Native regression tests cover retention, saturated rejection, and slot reuse after expiration. Physical headset validation remains outstanding.
