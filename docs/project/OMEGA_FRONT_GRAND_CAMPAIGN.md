# Omega Front — Seventy-Campaign Grand War
**Status:** **PROPOSED campaign design only; NO PLAYABLE MISSIONS IMPLEMENTED.** This is the first complete 70-part grand-campaign story and battle skeleton for the native game.
**Authority:** creator requires a semi-open-world war, **70 substantial faction campaigns**, confrontation with **all seventeen factions** and a **brutal Level 1→100** progression.
**Foundations:** [World Bible](OMEGA_FRONT_WORLD_BIBLE.md) · [Seventeen Factions](OMEGA_FRONT_FACTIONS.md) · [Faction Chronicles](OMEGA_FRONT_FACTION_CHRONICLES.md) · [Hearthwell Commander Prologue](OMEGA_FRONT_HEARTHWELL_PROLOGUE.md) · [Environment Design](OMEGA_FRONT_ENVIRONMENTS.md) · [Mechanics](WAR_WITHOUT_END.md)

## 1. A campaign, not seventy loosely connected battles

**70 faction campaigns** means seventy **separately tracked, substantial multi-mission operational arcs**, not seventy single skirmishes. Each arc contains at least **three unique consequential battles**, generally **three to eight**, plus some diplomacy, scouting, resupply, construction, escort or reconnaissance activity with real simulation consequences. The **minimum authored mainline is 210 consequential battles** if every arc has exactly three. Actual combat hours are far higher due to severe leveling, repeatable dynamic frontline battles, inspection expeditions, losses, optional operations and multi-stage sieges. Individual active engagements may be short; the campaign is deliberately long.

There are **17 major factions × four dedicated arcs = 68** plus **one extra Meridian Combine arc and one extra Hourless Regency arc = 70**. This is an exact auditable distribution. Each faction must be **fought in earnest at least once** on the strategic map; a diplomatic faction's armed detachment can be a secessionist, hostile field commander, territorial dispute or unavoidable clash without turning every civilian into a lawful target or requiring eradication. Faction survival, resettlement, treaties and alliance consequences persist. The player is an eighteenth **independent minor settlement** only in the colloquial sense; it is not an eighteenth member of the fixed principal-faction index.

The first cycle's 70 completed arcs earns the campaign's **standard major ending** and unlocks a verified **World Reclamation at Level 100** only when the *full existing covenant* is met (commander, designated active forces/equipment/formation/base/world/doctrine all Level 100). The **true ending remains gated behind at least ten complete World Reclamations**, ten corresponding Law Seals and the hidden ultimate confrontation. Do not collapse the two endings. On subsequent world cycles the 70 campaign arcs are substantially mutated by altered factions, laws, locations and player decisions—not silently skipped or represented only by health multipliers.

## 2. The leveling contract: miserable by design

The earlier mechanics charter specifies a benchmark for one mastery track: \`hours(n→n+1)=5.70084455×exp(0.0336257141×(n−1))\`, n=1..99. This measures *equal-threat effective active combat*, never calendar-gated timers or offline idle rewards. **Level 1→5 takes roughly 24 effective battle-hours and Level 95→100 roughly 720 hours** for the specified single track. Around 4,486 hours are implied for that isolated 1→100 track at baseline. Commander, unit, weapon, equipment, base, doctrine and world tracks can advance **concurrently from different earned actions**, so these are not additive player-time promises.

**A chapter's level window is a recommended threat/equipment bracket and narrative access stage, not free levels awarded on completion.** A player can unlock several candidate arcs while spending a very long period preparing. Missions award risk-and-deed-weighted XP and rare inputs, not enough XP by fiat to skip the grind. **The world must remain persistently dangerous even while the player farms.** Higher-level opponents react, forward bases change hands, convoys need actual escort and casualty extraction matters. Repeatable active operations vary tactical conditions so mastering a frontline is combat, not a single weak enemy clicked ten thousand times.

Late-stage advancement cannot be bypassed by replaying trivial targets. Equal-threat battle success, costly objective completion, commander participation, attrition and equipment mastery are measurable. Prestige inheritance can change the options and difficulty but must not quietly make Level 95→100 take five minutes.

## 3. Semi-open theater campaign orchestration

**Campaign structure:** each movement contains seven arcs and one coherent pressure event, but **three to five of that movement's seven arcs are selectable in flexible order** depending on scouting, routes, treaties and faction access. A couple of escalation arcs serve as dependency gates to keep consequences causally intelligible. The player also has meaningful optional fronts and rearguards at all times. First completion of a campaign sets a durable achievement and tactical history; enemy remnant forces and contested sectors remain active after its story resolution.

A campaign can be completed through more than one military approach—relief and interdiction, capture and extraction, or open siege—without evading the requirement for a real contested battle against that faction. **No empty 'click resolve diplomatically' option**. Diplomacy changes whose units are hostile, casualty thresholds, objectives and post-battle relationships, and may avoid genocide, but a war story still involves material military friction.

**Operations within each arc:** recommended 3–8 battles, including at least one maneuver encounter, one pressure/defense or interdiction encounter, and one pivotal objective battle. Most later arcs should include a field headquarters assault or an evolving multi-sector operation. Operations can be abandoned; recovery costs and the enemy's gained position are persistent.

**Theater state:** 600 strategic sectors across 12 theaters, with 16 named early Hearthfall sectors and many later procedural/curated nodes. Do not show only seven selectable chapter cards. The player navigates actual territory, logistics networks, patrols, contested routes, changing morphology, scouting and variable faction claims. Campaign availability arises from world conditions and intelligence, not solely a menu page number.

**Campaign world pressure:** the latest secure Eden lands draw migrants; factions can contest the same water/soil; the new Eden expands and retracts along forecasted morphology fronts. Civilian populations have food, water, evacuation and shelter requirements. Alliances give tangible access to depots and units. Each faction follows finite manpower/material/energy constraints and only what its intelligence knows.

**Progression ownership:** XP and equipment improvements from skirmishes, inspections and support/defense actions persist when campaigns change order. Every arc's risk rating derives from enemy force, supply reach, terrain, weather, preparation and current player level; do not autolevel every enemy to the player, which would erase earned advantages. The player may challenge overleveled areas early at real risk and may exploit mature forces against low threat, but low-threat farming gives drastically reduced relevant mastery.

## 5. Minimum sub-mission schema for all seventy

For each arc, production must deliver:
1. **Operational hook:** why a faction commits scarce resources here now; what it knows; who pays.
2. **Front stage:** where on the 600-sector map, required recon and legal/logistical approach, target threat bracket and openly available routes.
3. **First hostile contact:** actual battle involving the named primary faction, cover/LOS, objective and a nontrivial threat; not a fabricated victory dialog.
4. **Supply dilemma:** at least one material restriction—limited fuel, wounded soldiers, broken bridge, civilian convoy, ammunition production, unstable anomaly or urgent extraction.
5. **Enemy strategic response:** changes location, field forces, reinforcements or negotiation when the player wins or loses.
6. **Pivotal encounter:** boss formation, fortified headquarters, changing geography, siege or multi-sector command battle; has real defense and escape/retreat rules.
7. **After-action outcomes:** casualties, resources, sector control, civilian state, diplomacy, faction survival, named story facts, XP sources and equipment histories.
8. **Replayability:** deep repeatable frontline events driven by evolving faction economy, ecology, combat choices and intelligent responses, not an identical weak enemy loop.

Arc ID \`OF-C01\` through \`OF-C70\`; operation IDs \`OF-C01-M01\` etc. No mission-ID reuse after prestige. Recorded campaign completion uses \`cycle, arc_id, outcome_id, faction_id, world_seed, state_hash, completion_time_in_sim_ticks\`. A higher prestige's arc variants retain recognizable central identity and meaningful tactical changes.

## 7. What the commander earns and what remains uncertain

Beginning at **Level 1**, the commander is an emergency-appointed assistant operations chief protecting newly Edenized Hearthwell with a small field contingent. Their rise is through actual combat, competent withdrawals, defended civilians, earned troop loyalty, recovered veterans, functioning logistics and increasing strategic responsibility. Level 100 means they have earned command of a world-scale force; it does not mean all seventeen factions are dead or the civilians have become abstract production tokens.

The **Hearthwell Emergence** is the catalyst for war: land formerly classed as wasteland becomes naturally habitable, the landscape shifts and its changing boundaries trigger new overlapping legal, ecological and temporal claims. The true cause, planetary bisection physics, Ouroboros's purpose and missing Captain Ilyan Ro's fate **remain open mysteries**. The ending may disclose evidence without turning speculation into early-game fact. The tenfold prestige narrative can gradually answer approved questions.

## 8. Implementation and acceptance gates

**GC-01 — structure:** exactly 70 unique arc IDs, 17 unique faction IDs, each faction ≥4 arcs, F02 and F17 each 5, exactly 10 movements ×7, no dangling operation references.

**GC-02 — plot causality:** each military clash has a material or political cause; every arc's player choices and losses write persistent data; friendly factions can have genuine rival-command confrontations without compulsory massacre.

**GC-03 — open-world viability:** at least three simultaneously available meaningful frontlines during normal progression; absence of world-map softlock from treaties or lost convoy; reachable alternate route for essential story materials.

**GC-04 — battle fidelity:** each arc implemented as a minimum of three actual tactical operations and one meaningful faction-specific AI counter; raw level scaling alone does not meet the bar.

**GC-05 — grind calibration:** test effective-hours model for 1→5 and 95→100 (one mastery track) with real combat outcomes; independently prove XP can't be duplicated, inventory cannot be cloned and easy encounters do not defeat the intended late difficulty curve.

**GC-06 — full story:** one 70/70 completed first world cycle can qualify for level-100 reclamation only with covenant proof; nine reclamations cannot unlock the true ending; ten completed, valid cycles plus Law Seals and final hidden encounter can.

**GC-07 — mobile VR:** headless deterministic combat and story validation first; art maps, UI, stereo, controls, saves, 30–120-minute S9 thermal acceptance and one-APK packaging are separate evidence gates.

**Present reality:** this is a proposed **70-campaign game design**, **not seventy shipped campaigns**, a completed world simulator, an APK update, or a playable game.
