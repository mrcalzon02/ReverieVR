# Omega Front — Hearthwell Emergence, Commander Origin, and Opening Campaign
**Maturity:** Story / game-system design, **not implemented**. Creator-confirmed in this direction: a remote recently arisen Eden Zone formed from wasteland by shifting morphology, attracting competing factions; the player commands a small contingent. All character names, numbers, and exact events below are **proposed and revisable**.
**References:** [World Bible](OMEGA_FRONT_WORLD_BIBLE.md) · [Factions](OMEGA_FRONT_FACTIONS.md) · [Faction Chronicles](OMEGA_FRONT_FACTION_CHRONICLES.md) · [Environments](OMEGA_FRONT_ENVIRONMENTS.md) · [70 Campaigns](OMEGA_FRONT_GRAND_CAMPAIGN.md)

## 1. The land that wasn't there

A frontier survey dated only weeks before the campaign marked **Hearthfall March, Survey Grid Seven** as dead country. Scrapped freight lines led nowhere. An abandoned waterworks sat beneath toxic silt, and the soil was too caustic for unshielded skin. No major army had reason to occupy it.

Then the **Hearthwell Emergence** occurred. The morphology of the landscape shifted without a known mechanism. The aquifer moved. A burned dry channel became a flowing stream; poisonous mud was pushed aside by ordinary living soil; ruined roadbed rose into terraces. Long-silent orchard roots sprouted. The air could be breathed by a baseline human without a filter. Not an entire theater: a *patchwork frontier Eden* surrounded by the same hostile wastes, with uncertain boundaries and open rift scars nearby.

The event is not declared safe, divine, or engineered. Survey stakes sometimes return a few paces from where they were planted. A road that was navigable yesterday may be buried or displaced tomorrow. Most changes are slow enough for surveying, evacuation, construction, and counteraction, but occasional pulses cut off roads or reveal pockets of uninhabitable air. **Morphology is a meaningful, forecastable world system, not a license for arbitrary sudden defeat.**

Word of a newly born Eden is strategically incendiary. Existing maps define old ownership poorly. The waterworks' old deeds are lost. No established government can easily prove its authority. The soil is potentially priceless; so is an instrument that could explain how it happened.

The locals call the patch **Hearthwell** because warm, clean water was the first reliable proof it had changed. Its first residents call it *ours* because no one else would come when it was wasteland.

## 2. Why an insignificant contingent is there

**Proposed starting employer:** a small, independent **Boundary Survey Cooperative**, paid in supplies and passage rights to chart hazardous routes, mark viable wells, escort displaced families, and recover weathered civil-engineering records. It is not one of the 17 principal factions. It may have traded with Hearthward, Scrapline or Meridian without being their pawn. Its original contract explicitly concerned *wasteland reconnaissance*, not ownership of a new Eden Zone.

**Initial expedition size: 31 personnel**, deliberately modest:
- 12 armed guards and reconnaissance personnel (organized as three four-person teams).
- 6 surveyors and field engineers.
- 3 medical staff.
- 4 drivers, mechanics, and logistics personnel.
- 6 civilian specialists and volunteers (water/food/records/community liaison).

The commander begins without an aircraft carrier, armored army, national treasury, or reliable supply line. The force can field fewer than twelve guards at once because rest, observation, and camp defense still consume manpower. A handful of repurposed vehicles, ammunition boxes, a field radio, tents, a generator, and ration stocks are real assets. Early production should emerge from salvaged machinery, engineering skill, negotiated trade and viable local labor, not from a free 'build refinery' button.

**Origin principle:** the player did not arrive as the preordained savior. The contingent's assignment was low-prestige and dangerous. Nearly everyone important was deployed elsewhere on more lucrative fronts. The expedition happened to be near the changing ground when everybody suddenly wanted to own it.

## 3. How the protagonist becomes commander

The expedition originally answered to **Expedition Captain Ilyan Ro**, a proposed seasoned officer whose authority derived from the cooperative's signed field charter. During the Emergence, a shifting causeway separated the expedition command vehicle from its escort. Communications cut out. The captain and the command vehicle were last seen entering a strip of air that no longer aligns with the road.

**Ro is missing, not canonically dead.** A later search may find evidence, a rescue opportunity, an impostor, a time-displaced survivor or nothing. Do not choose a final answer during the tutorial.

The player character held a real but low command-grade role: **assistant operations chief and senior field organizer**. They were responsible for coordinating ration manifests, escort duty, survey routes and emergency withdrawal plans, not commanding a grand army. When the captain disappears, the highest remaining combat sergeant refuses to abandon the newly habitable survivors; the acting logistics officer cannot establish safe extraction; the survey lead has contradictory maps. The player is the one person with a complete picture of who is alive, where the ammunition is, which roads remain passable and whether the water can be defended.

A surviving field charter invokes **Emergency Continuity Clause Seven**: the personnel present choose an acting field commander until the cooperative reestablishes contact or a new civil compact replaces the charter. The appointment is recorded by signatures, not supernatural destiny. Two people dissent, one abstains, and the vote is narrow. The player gains legal authority over the **contingent**, not automatic ownership of the people, soil or future residents of Hearthwell.

This solves the level-one paradox: the player may have useful military experience but begins at **Command Mastery Level 1** because this is their first independent large-scale mandate under Omega Front progression rules. Early competencies remain credible; command authority, strategic doctrine and world-linked mastery start at the beginning.

### Player customization without erasing the premise

All origins serve as assistant operations chief; their prior specialty changes early play without creating separate incompatible stories:
1. **Quartermaster:** excellent inventories, salvage contracts and difficult withdrawal accounting; weaker offensive initiative.
2. **Escort Sergeant:** better troop cohesion and first-contact tactics; fewer initial industrial contacts.
3. **Field Engineer:** faster road/water repair and terrain reading; less political leverage.
4. **Relief Organizer:** extra civilian confidence, refugee logistics and medical stabilization; lower starting heavy equipment proficiency.

Use a customizable name/pronouns/appearance and one choice of prior specialty. **No 'secret Ouroboros heir', predetermined royal heritage, or last chosen child** is forced into the origin.

## 4. The provisional civil compact

Two days into the crisis, mechanics restore the first pump. On the third, the first family removes their respirators outside. By the fifth, strangers appear on the road with children and blankets. By the seventh, Meridian survey stakes stand at the edge of the terraces.

The player cannot legally claim the residents just because soldiers guard them. The new settlement forms a **Temporary Hearthwell Compact**, authorizing the contingent to provide protection in exchange for water, shelter, repair materials, recruitment access by consent and publicly accounted requisition. Civil stewardship exists alongside command. The compact contains an emergency council of a water technician, civilian quartermaster, resident delegate, medical representative and military commander.

This separation produces actual gameplay tension. A commander may order a tactical withdrawal. The council may refuse to abandon a well full of people. The player may requisition crops, but overrequisitioning destroys trust and future recruitment. A corporation offering guaranteed industrial water pumps may be attractive even if its legal claim is predatory.

At start, Hearthwell is an **independent minor settlement**, not an eighteenth major faction. It may become a recognized polity later, but its initial existence does not violate the world's 17-faction count. Factions may have local splinters without increasing the principal count.

## 5. Why the factions come

What the factions believe is not necessarily what is true:
- **F01 Hearthward League:** a chance to save more civilians, and an obligation to prevent monopolization of a new breathing place.
- **F02 Meridian Combine:** the old corporate salvage concession may, in its own interpretation, cover whatever emerges from this ground; the claim is legally disputed, but it comes with armored escorts.
- **F03 Last Standard Command:** border stabilization is necessary before the shifting land cuts off regional supply routes; the command fears uncontrolled militarization.
- **F04 Scrapline Freeholds:** roads and buried storage newly exposed by land movement may contain priceless components; scavengers demand finder rights.
- **F05 Pale Equation Institute:** nobody has seen a wasteland become Eden this quickly; fresh measurements may teach containment—or exploitation.
- **F06 Lost Ninth:** the aquifer and fields look like a place to feed legionary camp followers and build a permanent colony.
- **F07 Ashen Crown:** an unclaimed Eden basin is a legitimate new estate in its conception of authority.
- **F08 Copper Steppe Confederation:** reopened pasture and water could keep displaced herds alive and preserve seasonal migration.
- **F09 Brassline Republic:** the elevated roadbed hints at a route for industrial rails and artillery supply through contested country.
- **F10 Farwake:** shifting morphology may uncover technologies or conditions unavailable anywhere else; a refuge might help their small population survive.
- **F11 Ascendant Choir:** the environmental transition may reveal a compatible place to anchor a new augmented enclave or protect a vulnerable host population.
- **F12 Carrion Circuit:** buried vehicles, wreckage and metals raised by the Emergence are food for an expanding swarm.
- **F13 Red Bloom:** recovered soil and fresh water are an extraordinarily rich biological frontier.
- **F14 Quarry Directive:** new exposed strata constitute an unprocessed extraction zone; the machines may attempt to erase the Eden topsoil to reach ore.
- **F15 Vitreous Court:** fresh spatial discontinuities are attractive territory for anomalous geometry and anchor structures.
- **F16 Pilgrim Road:** a new place to settle survivors who have nowhere in this era to go; migration can help Hearthwell prosper or overwhelm its capacity.
- **F17 Hourless Regency:** the land has shifted in time and may fall under a claimed temporal jurisdiction. The claim's factual validity is unknown.

**Information propagation:** most distant factions initially receive rumors, intercepted corporate surveys or refugee testimony. They cannot be present on day one. Their first agents should arrive as campaign pressure and exploration expand.

## 6. Named local figures (all PROPOSED, none fixed player protagonists)

- **Acting commander: the player.** Flexible name/specialty. Their legitimacy rests on the emergency charter and the compact's continuing confidence.
- **Captain Ilyan Ro:** missing original leader. Their fate is a long-form mystery, not an introduction spoiler.
- **Deputy Tamsin Vale:** pragmatic senior squad sergeant; distrusts anyone who calls retreat cowardice when civilians are in danger.
- **Edda Moor:** water engineer, representative of the provisional compact. Her priority is that a captured well still produces drinkable water after the shooting.
- **Mara Sen:** Hearthward watch-surveyor with a sincere offer of help and a preference for shared stewardship.
- **Adjunct Corven Pell:** Meridian contractual claims officer whose manners are impeccable and whose annexation documents are alarmingly thorough.
- **Rook Ferris:** Scrapline road salvager with a disputed ownership ledger and the means to keep broken vehicles running.
- **Centurion Cassian Varro:** Lost Ninth envoy, proud but not stupid, responsible for both soldiers and starving camp followers.
- **Recorder Silex-Three:** uncertain F05 research voice assembled from incomplete anomaly observations; may be a title, operator or system, not an explanation of the Rupture.
- **The Wreck-Choir:** survivors' nickname for F12 machine communications picked up around S013; does not imply true biological sentience or a faction-wide leader.

Personality conflicts should be resolved by player decisions and persistent records. Do not write an ally's unconditional scripted betrayal merely because the plot needs combat.

## 7. Opening cinematic and first interactive hour (PROPOSED)

**Cold open:** rain on the roof of an old truck. A survey tablet displays a red WASTELAND classification, last verified several weeks ago. The viewpoint passes a dead orchard, a rusted bridge and a valley where green leaves now move in a clean wind. Someone removes their mask. The radio cuts out. The road behind the expedition does not match its own survey map.

> CAPTAIN RO: "This isn't our valley."
>
> EDDA MOOR: "It is now. Listen to the water."
>
> [A distant detonation. The second vehicle's transmission fails.]
>
> SERGEANT VALE: "Where is the command truck?"
>
> [Three broken transmissions, none intelligible.]
>
> VOICE FROM THE REAR: "We're cut off. The road was *there*."
>
> [The player opens the personnel ledger, assigns survivors to cover the pump and begins the first real tactical operation.]

*Copy editing:* the spoken name Edda Moor is capitalized as a name in script when implemented; cinematic lines are prose draft, not shipped VO assets.

**Opening action A — muster:** inspect units and inventories, assign damaged rifles, triage a wounded driver, choose which four people escort a pump team. The important early choice is *who is left to guard civilians while the player deploys*.

**Action B — clear a route:** a short real-time-with-tactical-pause battle against unaligned raiders, or a hazard-driven rescue (depending on chosen tutorial variant). Win by clearing a convoy lane and bringing guards home with sufficient ammunition, not by killing everything.

**Action C — establish the compact:** the water technician and sergeant support acting command. The player chooses an initial policy on confiscation, refugee access and emergency service. These choices produce ledger values and faction attitudes, not locked story rails.

**Action D — detect claims:** F01 offers a water-test kit, F04 requests a salvage survey, F02 files a claim; a stranger carries a Roman-style camp seal. Player chooses which contact to answer first. At least three valid operation starts remain selectable on a compact scrollable theater interface.

**First-hour technical gate:** the game must let the player issue a real tactical order, observe a consequential terrain hazard, allocate finite resources, lose and recover units, negotiate or refuse one faction request and view a persistent ledger of consequences. The user must never be shown campaign choices that haven't been implemented.

## 8. Shifting morphology as a simulated conflict

**State per sector:** `morphology_epoch, prior_ecology, current_ecology, stability, transition_forecast, emergence_cells, road_displacement, water_table, habitable_air, anomaly_pressure, surveyed_confidence, displaced_structures, civilian_exposure`.

**Transition event:** when morphology updates, terrain may change elevation/cover/roads/water, infrastructure may require repairs, and habitability is reassessed with actual measurable data. Eden classification emerges **only** when baseline-human atmosphere and environmental requirements are genuinely satisfied. A clean spring with lethal ambient air is not Eden.

**Warnings:** seismic cues, growth rates, survey differences, air/water instruments, refugee testimony and faction intelligence predict imminent changes. A player can proactively reroute convoys, move civilians, stabilize a field or deliberately exploit a forecast enemy supply break. No untelegraphed full-army deletion.

**Conflict escalation:** every new stable Eden tile raises the regional settlement and recruitment potential but also increases the strategic claim value of Hearthwell; enemies learn from public changes at their own information speed. A new bridge can suddenly create the shortest invasion route; a severed bridge may block a relief convoy. The player must maintain the local map, not merely paint conquered hexes green.

**Ambiguous cause:** plausible in-universe explanations include self-healing world, localized reality-state swapping, ancient infrastructure activation or deliberate interference, but **none is accepted as the true cause**. This is the central mystery of the first world cycle.

## 9. The commander's long road: 70 faction campaigns, Level 1–100

The full proposed grand campaign is specified in [OMEGA_FRONT_GRAND_CAMPAIGN.md](OMEGA_FRONT_GRAND_CAMPAIGN.md). It is structured as **70 substantial faction-led arcs**, each containing multiple battles/operations, with every one of the seventeen factions confronted militarily and revisited across later ages. Story flags track alliances and civilian outcomes so a genuine armed clash need not mean exterminating a sympathetic people.

The first world-cycle completion ends the 70-part campaign with its standard major ending and Level-100 world-covenant eligibility. The previous **at-least-ten Level-100 complete world prestiges** remain required for the ultimate true ending; the 70-campaign structure is reused with changed doctrines, laws, opponents and geography in subsequent cycles, not skipped by selecting an ending from a menu.

## 10. Revision boundaries

The player commander isn't canonically tied by blood to Ouroboros, wasn't secretly a level-100 hero before the campaign and does not own Hearthwell by simple military appointment. Hearthwell's new Eden condition is creator direction; its exact geography, morphology rules, charter politics, character names and cause remain proposed pending approval. The absence of an explanation is deliberate.

**Verification state:** narrative and mechanics-design prose only. None of the cinematic, commander backgrounds, morphology engine, faction dialog, 70 campaign arcs or gameplay are source-implemented by this document.
