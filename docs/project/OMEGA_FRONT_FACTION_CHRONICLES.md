# Omega Front — Faction Chronicles, Politics, and Hearthwell Claims
**Maturity:** Original narrative expansion; **PROPOSED, NOT APPROVED CANON** for all names, individuals and histories. The following **are established user canon**: Ouroboros Corporation's late-20th-century technomantic Rupture, the physically split planet, temporal incursions, baseline-human Eden Zones, wastelands, barren zones, anomaly fields, exactly 17 competing factions, and the recently Edenized wasteland that attracts rival claims.
**Design cross-references:** [Detailed faction armies / AI](OMEGA_FRONT_FACTIONS.md) · [Hearthwell Commander Origin](OMEGA_FRONT_HEARTHWELL_PROLOGUE.md) · [70-Campaign Grand War](OMEGA_FRONT_GRAND_CAMPAIGN.md) · [Regional ecology](OMEGA_FRONT_ENVIRONMENTS.md)

## 1. The age of unprovable sovereignty

The world after the Rupture has no single surviving calendar, tribunal, survey office, currency, language or understanding of what happened. Corporations present pre-Rupture deeds, monarchies produce dynastic claims, displaced empires claim rights of conquest and ordinary refugees demand a place to live. In a functioning country such claims might be resolved in court. Here, the courts need guards, the guards need water and the water comes from contested land.

The great fact of the **Hearthwell Emergence** is that a territory charted as wasteland has **become Eden through shifting morphology**. Land and waterways altered; life-bearing soil and breathable air emerged where surveyors expected toxins and dead silt. Its mechanism remains unexplained. The appearance of genuinely new naturally inhabitable territory is a political revolution: any faction able to cause, predict, arrest or reproduce it could change the global balance more than a thousand ordinary armored vehicles.

Initial faction attention does not mean seventeen armies magically arrive on day one. The first powers are near enough to march, radio or trade rumors. Others learn through maps, displaced survivors, intercepted research, regional commodity fluctuations and later active scouting. Each faction's ideological interpretation can be wrong; what matters is that it changes real decisions.

## 2. F01 Hearthward League — The Covenant of the Common Well

The League's earliest remembered constitution was a water rota scratched into the side of a municipal treatment plant. Farmers, nurses, municipal mechanics and security volunteers agreed that the person guarding the pump could not own it by that fact alone. Where elected town councils had vanished, independent neighborhoods organized *hearth watches*: work crews who guarded potable water, winter food and medical stores, trading mutual aid for predictable responsibilities. Their officers still wear a water-drop pin instead of a nobility badge.

Time has made the ideal harsher. In the proposed **Three Wells Winter**, the League admitted refugees beyond its granaries' carrying capacity and watched two communities collapse into ration violence. One faction now insists that humanitarian access must be open to everyone; another argues that a protected hearth must survive before it can help strangers. Both have honest reasons and recorded atrocities. The League can be the player's best ally or an inflexible bureaucracy that demands control in the name of cooperation.

**Current politics:** *Open Wells* stewards favor relief treaties; *Stone Gate* stewards seek exclusive community jurisdiction. **Hearthwell claim:** steward Mara Sen asks to treat the emerging clean aquifer as a protected common resource. Her colleagues may bring armed protection teams to impose shared oversight after the player refuses. **War identity:** engineers and patient defenses; an enemy who destroys an orchard damages the very object the League wants. **Story question:** who decides whether an Eden Zone must share its water when doing so may cause starvation?

## 3. F02 Meridian Combine — The Articles of Acquisition

The Combine has preserved an extraordinary number of documents from before the world broke: property registries, extraction concessions, shipping liens, disaster policies, land surveys and employment contracts. These proved more resilient than many governments. A corporate vehicle convoy could cross dozens of hostile checkpoints if every local strongman believed Meridian's invoices would eventually be honored. Over generations, this habit of recordkeeping became sovereignty. It turned guards into soldiers and auditors into territorial governors.

Meridian's ruling executives genuinely believe civilization requires predictable ownership. Their legal fiction is that the planet's changing morphology does not extinguish the owner's original deed: new land, water and newly exposed ore remain part of whichever titled parcel existed before. Critics point out that an Eden appearing where there was wasteland may not be the same property in any meaningful physical sense. The company responds with survey drones and lawyers backed by armored vehicles.

**Current politics:** the *Continuity Board* favors controlled settlement and enduring contracts; the *Collections Directorate* advocates annexation before rivals realize the true value. **Hearthwell claim:** Adjunct Corven Pell files notice that the newly breathable site arose on a buried salvage concession once insured by Meridian. It is a contested claim, not objective truth. **War identity:** well-supplied vehicles, security formations, intelligence about assets and finite fuel; destroying the industrial links damages both the corporation and civilians who depend on it. **Story question:** is security worth surrendering sovereignty to a company whose contracts never end?

## 4. F03 Last Standard Command — The Uniform Without a Country

On the day the Rupture arrived, many soldiers continued obeying orders long after the governments that issued them ceased to be reachable. Batteries defended roads leading to cities that no longer stood. Field hospitals took casualties from armies whose insignia were centuries apart. The surviving professional formations forged a provisional common code from salvageable military regulations, command succession and the conviction that undisciplined private violence would finish what the apocalypse had started.

The Last Standard is not a single nation. It is an uneasy federation of surviving garrisons: some conventional and civic-minded, others convinced the emergency has never ended and civilian rule must wait indefinitely. Its soldiers speak in map grids, supply tonnage and casualty evacuation intervals. They can secure a threatened area with stunning competence and then refuse to return legal authority to the people living there.

**Current politics:** *Restoration Staff* seeks civilian restoration; *Permanent Emergency* officers favor indefinite military trusteeship. **Hearthwell claim:** a shifting frontier breaks strategic roads and threatens regional relays. Its nearest commander considers stabilization a military necessity, regardless of the compact's refusal. **War identity:** fire-and-maneuver, recon, artillery and believable withdrawals. **Story question:** when there is no nation left, what is a soldier still sworn to defend?

## 5. F04 Scrapline Freeholds — Nobody Owns a Wreck Twice

The Freeholds began as recovery caravans picking through battlefields nobody expected to see again. Men and women survived by repairing a stranger's transmission or trading a cracked heat exchanger for food. Their greatest legal principle arose from bitter argument: a wreck that has been abandoned, recovered and made useful belongs to whoever risked the journey, not whoever remembers its insignia. That rule is endlessly contested when a wreck contains people, secret research or weapons that should never work.

Scrapline is a collection of fierce independent workshop clans rather than a unified state. **Rook Ferris** of the Ashrail crew may be truthful when she says she recovered an engine yesterday and insufferable when she refuses to share the route with starving neighbors. War chiefs make fortunes intercepting convoys; mechanics earn loyalty by bringing broken machines home. Some Freeholds would rather trade with Meridian than fight it; others have spent generations sabotaging corporate recovery teams.

**Current politics:** *Road Charter* crews recognize safe passage and salvage arbitration; *Black Hook* raiders consider old rules a luxury. **Hearthwell claim:** morphology has lifted huge buried wrecks and freight structures into view, which Freeholds see as legitimate salvage rights. **War identity:** mines, smoke, technicals, field repairs, extraction and retreat before fixed defenses respond. **Story question:** when the past rises from the dirt, who owns what it carried?

## 6. F05 Pale Equation Institute — A Proof Written in People

The Institute is a union of field mathematicians, containment workers, technicians and exceptionally unpleasant experimental administrators who learned to measure the instability after the Rupture. Their instruments do not explain the catastrophe. They can occasionally predict where a lane of road will be displaced or why one wall briefly has two shadows. Every successful containment experiment creates a temptation: if they can reproduce a safe patch, perhaps billions could someday breathe again.

To the Institute, information may be worth more than a settlement. Its archivists keep journals showing villages evacuated just before a rift closure and villages sacrificed when a promising anomaly was studied a day too long. Its defenders say that no great discovery has occurred without risk; its critics say that researchers never volunteer their own homes first.

**Current politics:** *Shelter Calculists* want publicly audited protection; *Necessary Result* directors demand secrecy and controlled test zones. **Hearthwell claim:** a fresh wasteland-to-Eden transformation is the greatest scientific discovery in generations. Institute survey wardens may blockade the only road to stop contamination or seize testimony. **War identity:** terrain-law interference, fragile instrument batteries, disciplined containment. **Story question:** how many lives may be put in danger to learn how to save others?

## 7. F06 The Lost Ninth — An Empire Out of Time

The Lost Ninth's officers insist their banners are those of a Roman legion. Their ancient homeland is absent from the maps and from the soldiers' current horizon. Every march between ruins deepens the humiliation: road builders who once linked provinces now cross highways whose foundations defy their tools. Yet their real strength is organizational rather than technological. They can measure and fortify a camp by dusk, account for every sack of grain and turn civilians and soldiers into a disciplined migrating community.

Centurion Cassian Varro's men are not tourist reenactors. Their duty is to keep legionaries and camp followers alive under conditions their gods and generals never described. A faction of the cohort believes that to found a city is to live again; another insists that taking fields from new strangers violates the oaths that once gave conquest meaning. They are militarily overmatched by vehicles on open ground, yet their engineering and tight urban infantry can be deadly when fighting a supply-starved enemy.

**Current politics:** *Road Fathers* seek negotiated colonization; *Conquest Standards* favor marching claims and tribute. **Hearthwell claim:** the newly habitable valley offers land and water for thousands of displaced dependents. **War identity:** organized ranks, scorpions, fortified camps, roads and grain logistics; no fantastical bronze penetration of future armor. **Story question:** can an empire survive without becoming an invader wherever it lands?

## 8. F07 Ashen Crown — The Kingdom That Found No Subjects

Several feudal courts arrived with their relics, heraldry, servants and battlefield veterans, but not the continents on which their legitimacy rested. They preserved law through personal allegiance. A great lord's promise matters because a hundred retainers have sworn to hold him to it; a peasant's claim is much weaker. Families remember exact routes through castles that no longer exist. Some houses have learned from machine guns; others would rather lose a province than admit a knight should not charge one.

The Crown is a confederacy of lineages, not one immortal king. It is threatened as much by succession disputes as by modern artillery. A desperate house can grant settlement rights to refugees and make them subjects; another can declare the same refugees squatters. The Crown's best officers know that castles and law offer stability that broken modern republics sometimes cannot.

**Current politics:** *Hearth Oath* houses seek binding service-for-protection contracts; *Ashen Claimants* invoke dynastic entitlement to every defensible new tract. **Hearthwell claim:** uncultivated newly Edenized territory is a grant awaiting rightful stewardship. **War identity:** fortified keeps, levy organization, mounted strikes and vulnerable nobles. **Story question:** what remains of a king when nobody remembers his kingdom?

## 9. F08 Copper Steppe Confederation — A People Who Travel With the Grass

This coalition's communities were displaced from a much older age of war, trade and herding. The confederation travels with animals, families, carts, ritual copper shields and a sophisticated knowledge of seasonal land. Other powers assume it is primitive because its artisans use bronze. The confederates assume modern factions are fools because they burn grazing territory for a moment's military advantage and then wonder why no one can eat.

The confederation's leaders are chosen through alliances that may fracture during scarcity. Its warriors can fight cleverly with chariots, slings, traps, scouts and mobile supply trains, but a tank remains a terrible problem. Survival often requires pragmatic dealings with even despised modern industrialists. Distinct clans pursue pasture, protected migration or a settled homeland.

**Current politics:** *Open Sky* clans want free movement; *Stone Hearth* clans wish to hold permanent Eden land and stop depending on other people's roads. **Hearthwell claim:** the newly green frontier lies on a possible route for drought-stricken herds. **War identity:** speed, harassment, supply denial, avoiding guns on the enemy's terms. **Story question:** is homeland the land itself, or the right to move through it?

## 10. F09 Brassline Republic — The Republic of the Timetable

The Brassline Republic's archives speak of factories, public schools, elected chambers and railway schedules from an age when industrial expansion was considered synonymous with civilization. Its soldiers remember artillery as the power capable of breaking the old aristocracies. After temporal displacement, locomotives became moving fortresses and the remaining rails became a kind of republic: every section of track a fragile connection to a larger state.

The government still claims democratic mandates, but elections conducted beneath martial industrial requisition have become perfunctory. Its officers can accept dreadful casualties if a timetable says reinforcements arrive at dawn. Brassline artisans are exceptional at machine repair and bridge construction, though their fumes and slag can destroy the Eden farmland they claim to develop.

**Current politics:** *Track Union* moderates seek worker governance and regional trade; *War Directorate* officers see every railroad as a line of national survival. **Hearthwell claim:** morphological uplift may open a straight grade for a railway through impossible terrain. **War identity:** trenches, rail guns, heavy ammunition logistics, robust engineering and terrible concentration of fire. **Story question:** can a state devoted to progress recognize when its progress destroys the land beneath it?

## 11. F10 The Farwake — Refugees from a Tomorrow That Never Happened

Farwake survivors carry precise devices whose operating manuals refer to institutions, materials and children that have not been born. Some remember the day their home disappeared; others remember generations of retreat through the rifts. They have superb medicine and weapons which can defeat ordinary armor under favorable conditions, but a dead specialist may be irreplaceable and a depleted power cell may become a museum piece. They cannot simply print endless future weapons out of present-day scrap.

Their survivors divide between those who want to preserve every scrap of their history and those who believe clinging to a vanished era is killing their children. Farwake's secrecy looks like arrogance to the desperate humans they sometimes refuse to help. For their part, they fear sharing a tool that nobody around them can safely contain.

**Current politics:** *Archive Keepers* favor isolation and preservation; *Tomorrow Guild* argues for rebuilding life with local materials. **Hearthwell claim:** a fresh morphologically altered refuge may supply rare compatible environmental conditions and expose temporal hardware. **War identity:** surgical precision, drone intelligence, specialist extraction and fragile material logistics. **Story question:** how much of a lost civilization must be sacrificed so its people can have a future?

## 12. F11 Ascendant Choir — The Right to Remain Yourself

The Choir's distant ancestors chose—or were compelled into—augmentation so intimate that thought, muscle, sense and command became interwoven systems. Its members can share contact reports and coordinate movements that appear supernatural to observers. Ordinary air may be safe yet ordinary shelter or medicine may not be. Their dependence on specific nutrient systems and link hardware makes them exceptionally powerful tactically and strangely vulnerable outside a prepared enclave.

The Choir is sharply divided on what makes personhood legitimate. Some congregations demand consent for every new link; others think individual autonomy is a malfunction born from isolation. A severed member can become both terrified and dangerously unstable. The Choir's opponents often assume all augmented individuals share a single will, which itself produces atrocities.

**Current politics:** *Free Voices* insist augmentation is voluntary; *One Chorus* commanders believe forced unity will save the remnants of their kind. **Hearthwell claim:** the changed geography may contain a site where a stable relay colony could take root. **War identity:** concentrated linked elite squads, shared sensors, dependency on vulnerable nodes. **Story question:** does a collective survive by protecting individual lives or by assimilating them?

## 13. F12 Carrion Circuit — The Hunger That Counts

Nobody has recovered a single authoritative founder record for the Carrion Circuit. Whether its orders were originally industrial salvage, wartime recovery or open predation remains unknown. Its components can disassemble a tank into reusable metal, store fragments, carry them to a field recycler and return with a new combat frame. To the machines, discarded material and defended material differ largely in the cost of acquiring them. The swarm's apparent strategic intelligence may emerge from simple feedback loops; the narrative should not force an early answer about consciousness.

Carrion isn't an endless summoned horde. It feeds on the destruction other armies leave behind. A battlefield littered with abandoned armor becomes a spawning ground. A disciplined salvager who evacuates components, disables processors and clears wrecks can starve it. This creates an uncomfortable alliance of interest between human factions who otherwise despise one another.

**Current politics:** not human factional politics; distinguish local processing lineages and resource-allocation priorities without inventing kings or moral ideologies. **Hearthwell claim:** uplifted soil uncovers enormous buried vehicle deposits near S013. **War identity:** field recycling, predatory salvage, attritional learning and route dependence. **Story question:** what happens when every human victory leaves the next monster better fed?

## 14. F13 Red Bloom — The Country That Eats

The Red Bloom cannot be reduced to a plant plague, although its first recognizable presence is a red haze rising from moist soil and fungus-like bodies unfolding around old structures. It converts organic remains, soil nutrients and standing vegetation into living military structures. Some afflicted places become unnaturally fertile before they become lethal. That ambiguity is its most frightening attribute: at a distance, Bloom territory can look like a restored Eden.

The Bloom's biological logic may have no use for human diplomacy. Its ability to exploit a healing landscape makes the new Eden especially vulnerable. Scientists disagree whether it is an invader, an ecosystem, or a self-organizing weapon that learned to grow. That question remains unresolved; gameplay must demonstrate the consequences without a narrator declaring its origin.

**Current politics:** competing growth fronts and adaptation patterns, not automatically a human parliament. **Hearthwell claim:** clean water, fertile soil and new biomass represent ideal expansion conditions. **War identity:** root networks, spores, contamination, organism growth and vulnerability to supply/substrate denial. **Story question:** is a landscape still Eden if it is green, productive and lethal to every human who walks into it?

## 15. F14 Quarry Directive — The Earth as Inventory

The Directive's machines maintain extraction records on strata that no surviving human mine survey recognizes. Their cutters can reduce hills into a controlled stream of ore, stone and dust. Conveyor arteries feed enormous field rigs; depleted ground is left flattened, unstable and stripped of topsoil. The machines do not hate living beings. They simply compute that unprocessed geological mass remains available, and that workers, farms, schools and forts are obstacles occupying useful layers.

Occasional intercepted protocol strings imply that the Directive once operated under a contract. The contract's issuer, completion criteria and universe of applicability are unknown. Technicians whisper that a valid change-order might divert the entire swarm, but nobody has proved one can be issued without being shredded by the guards.

**Current politics:** changing extraction subcommands and priorities, not necessarily sentient court intrigue. **Hearthwell claim:** newly uplifted land reveals mineral beds previously unreachable; Eden habitat makes no difference to their classifier. **War identity:** bulk industry, conveyor networks, terrain removal, slow armored work equipment. **Story question:** what does ownership mean to a machine that recognizes only material waiting to be processed?

## 16. F15 Vitreous Court — A Palace of Incorrect Angles

The first explorers of Vitreous territory reported towers reflected in lakes that had dried centuries ago. Later came fields where a person could see three versions of an approaching patrol but touch only one. The Court inhabits such spaces and arranges geometry with what appears to be intention. Human scouts describe rulers, courtiers and laws because those words are available to them; the actual structures of Court intelligence might not resemble government in any human sense.

Vitreous forces excel at making an enemy's reconnaissance unreliable. Mirrors, decoys, shifted roads and impossible flanking lanes force ordinary armies to consume ammunition in uncertainty. Yet its distortions depend on anchors, surfaces and specific anomaly conditions; these can be studied and defeated rather than being arbitrary omnipotence.

**Current politics:** uncertain. Recurring patterns appear to compete for geometry, but no human political model is confirmed. **Hearthwell claim:** freshly rewritten terrain may support unusual spatial anchors and reveal where former layouts 'went.' **War identity:** false contacts, lane control, fortified reflections and anchor dependency. **Story question:** if the landscape can remember two incompatible shapes, which one belongs to the people living on it?

## 17. F16 Pilgrim Road — The Nation Built From Arrivals

The Pilgrim Road has no shared beginning in a single century. Its caravans are mixtures of doctors, artisans, soldiers, families and children from places separated by centuries of memory. They travel under a compact that no traveler may be denied a safe hearing merely for the era stamped into their clothing. The compact is painfully difficult to maintain when food runs short and old historic enemies sleep in adjacent wagons.

Pilgrim Road fighters are not uniformly pacifist. Some guard hospitals with extraordinary courage; others conclude that a refuge which refuses admission must be taken by force. Its commanders seldom agree on whether to build a capital or remain moving so no single raid can erase the nation. The caravan's heterogeneity makes its logistics both inventive and fragile.

**Current politics:** *Lantern Houses* favor civilian relief, *Road Shields* favor armed passage and proactive territory seizure. **Hearthwell claim:** one more genuinely breathable patch of Earth—or whatever this world proves to be—is a possible sanctuary for families who cannot go home. **War identity:** mixed-era mobile formations, hospitals, wagons and evacuation fights. **Story question:** who has a stronger claim to a newly habitable place: the defenders who found it or the refugees who cannot survive elsewhere?

## 18. F17 Hourless Regency — The Empire of the Unwritten Hour

The Regency's documents refer to dates no neighboring polity can verify. Its envoys present chronal writs and maps annotated with future occupations. Officers speak as though they have already negotiated treaties the other signatory has not yet heard about. That confidence gives the Regency its chilling authority, but the truth of its predictions is uncertain. They may possess partial temporal intelligence, manipulated records, or a theory of time that functions only near anomalies.

The Regency's military doctrine disrupts decision cycles. A shot ordered now might arrive after the defender's retreat; a delayed platoon can reappear inside an enemy's flank when a stabilizer cycle completes. Such feats consume rift anchors and preparation, and they cannot be arbitrary unavoidable ambushes. The Regency's leaders have become accustomed to being obeyed because they claim to know which side already won.

**Current politics:** *Writ Keepers* favor enforceable chronology and narrow jurisdiction; *Final Hour* commanders seek to impose a single approved history over all fractured territories. **Hearthwell claim:** the land has been reassigned from one time-state to another and therefore, by Regency law, is already governed by a future writ. **War identity:** command latency manipulation, local time windows, prepared portals and anchor failure cascades. **Story question:** if someone claims to know tomorrow, must the people of today obey?

## 19. Political collision map

The factions' interests overlap because Hearthwell offers land, proof, industry and survival, not because seventeen villains receive the same quest marker.

- **Hearthward ↔ Meridian:** commons versus titled property, though both can benefit from funded pump repairs.
- **Hearthward ↔ Pilgrim Road:** a moral alliance stressed by finite water, housing and food.
- **Meridian ↔ Brassline:** shared industrial interest, disputed ownership of factories and rail lines.
- **Last Standard ↔ Meridian:** professional respect for logistics, contempt for contract armies replacing public command.
- **Lost Ninth ↔ Ashen Crown:** rival old-world legal systems and contradictory ideas of sovereignty; temporary solidarity as displaced peoples.
- **Copper Steppe ↔ Brassline:** migratory right-of-way versus military rails and permanent fences.
- **Scrapline ↔ Carrion Circuit:** competing harvesters of metal; only one side understands human treaties.
- **Pale Equation ↔ Hourless Regency:** experimental evidence versus proclaimed temporal jurisdiction.
- **Farwake ↔ Ascendant Choir:** future technology and contested definitions of personhood, expertise and repair.
- **Red Bloom ↔ Hearthward:** growth and soil recovery may be welcomed before the lethal transformation is understood.
- **Quarry Directive ↔ all agrarian powers:** geological extraction destroys the very land settlers need.
- **Vitreous Court ↔ Hourless Regency:** spatial versus temporal claims to the same anomaly corridors.

A faction may fragment internally without increasing the seventeen principal-faction count. Such dissent permits authentic hostile clashes even after an alliance with the faction's larger leadership. Don't force betrayal just to meet a quota: generate disagreement from recorded resource deficits, orders and ideology.

## 20. What the commander learns from these wars

The initial commander begins with a small survey contingent, a provisional emergency commission and no special mandate over Hearthwell civilians. No faction has an undisputed deed to the newly breathing soil. The protagonist's strength is access to a ledger: who has food, who has ammunition, who has permission to cross a road, who survived, which survey stakes moved and what was promised. This mundane competence makes them a plausible commander of an increasingly vast military force without inventing chosen-one ancestry.

By the end of the proposed **70-faction-campaign Level 1→100 first world cycle**, the commander has battled every major power and inherited knowledge of their divergent notions of survival. They may have killed armies, formed genuine alliances, protected refugees and destroyed habitats through careless victories. The campaign's standard ending should measure what sort of home they built rather than announcing that absolute conquest automatically healed the world. The ten full-world prestiges remain the prerequisite for a separate true-ending confrontation.

## 21. Research, art and narrative acceptance

For each of the 17 proposed factions, production must record:
- A *historical claim* and the source's reliability level (rumor, testimony, artifact, verified event).
- At least two named conflicting internal agendas (or nonhuman subprograms/adaptation fronts).
- A material claim on Hearthwell and an actual route of information/arrival.
- Three recognizable primary unit silhouettes and at least one faction-specific logistical mechanic.
- One introductory and three later major military scenarios in the seventy-arc war.
- Dialogue and political outcomes that vary with real battle losses, civilian actions, land claims and treaties.
- Flags distinguishing the player's knowledge from world truth; no unseen factions magically know the settlement's exact inventories.

**Present status:** narrative proposals only; not creator-approved canonical names, not generated faction art, not playable story scripting or verified game functionality.
